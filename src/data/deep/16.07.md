# Design: a social feed

A social feed combines relationship data, posts, ranking, and fast reads for users following many authors. The main scaling decision is how to build each viewer's candidate timeline. Fanout-on-write pushes a new post into followers' inboxes, making reads cheap but amplifying writes for accounts with huge audiences. Fanout-on-read fetches posts from followed authors at request time, making writes cheap but increasing read fanout and tail latency. A hybrid usually handles ordinary users with write fanout and celebrity accounts with read-time merge.

## Post creation and timeline projection

The post service validates content, privacy, and media state, then commits a post record and an outbox event. A fanout worker reads the event, determines eligible followers, and writes post IDs into timeline partitions. The post itself remains in canonical storage; timeline entries are lightweight references with ranking features and expiry or removal policy. Make fanout idempotent because queue delivery can repeat. Deletes, blocks, privacy changes, and moderation removals need propagation to both the post store and timeline caches; authorization must still be checked at read time for sensitive content.

For read-time merge, fetch recent posts from followed accounts and combine them with precomputed timeline entries. A time-based merge is simple, while ranked feeds need candidate generation and scoring by recency, relevance, relationship, and quality. Avoid making one slow followee or model service block the whole feed. Use deadlines, per-source caps, and a recent chronological fallback. Pagination needs a stable cursor containing sort position and tie-breaker; if ranking changes between pages, decide whether duplicates or skips are acceptable or provide a snapshot boundary.

## Scale and product integrity

Follower graphs and inboxes can be sharded by user. Large fanout jobs should be paged and throttled, and newly followed accounts may require backfill of recent posts. Hot authors should not create an unbounded synchronous write. A feed cache can store the first page, but invalidation and personalization make a single shared response unlikely. Cache candidate IDs and hydrate content separately. Keep media delivery on a CDN and avoid loading every media asset before the user scrolls.

Ranking signals can include user interaction, freshness, affinity, media type, and integrity risk. Safety and privacy constraints must filter candidates before ranking. Log impressions and position to train models without treating unseen posts as dislikes. Apply diversity so one author or format does not dominate. Users should be able to switch to chronological order or understand major feed controls. Moderation state and block relationships must be fresh enough to avoid showing harmful or unwanted content.

Measure feed latency by stage, candidate coverage, fanout lag, cache hit rate, duplicate or missing posts, freshness, hide/report rate, and user satisfaction. Test celebrity fanout, follow/unfollow races, post deletion during pagination, privacy changes, stale ranking features, and a fanout queue backlog. A feed is reliable when it remains useful under partial failures and respects relationship and privacy changes.

## Design review

Which users use write fanout and which use read-time merge? Are post writes and fanout intent atomic? How do deletes, blocks, and privacy changes reach cached timelines? Can ranking fail into a useful fallback? What cursor semantics apply while ranking changes?
