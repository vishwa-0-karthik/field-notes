# Design: a notification system

A notification system turns product events into messages delivered through email, SMS, push, or in-app channels. It should reliably accept notification intent, respect user preferences and legal constraints, fan out to many recipients when required, and track provider outcomes without claiming that provider acceptance means a person saw the message. Keep product event creation separate from channel delivery so a slow provider does not block checkout, messaging, or account workflows.

## Intake, policy, and fanout

The source service writes its business change and a notification event atomically, often with an outbox. A dispatcher reads the outbox or durable event log and creates notification jobs with stable IDs. Template and policy services determine recipients, locale, channel preference, quiet hours, and suppression rules. The policy decision should be versioned or snapshotted into the job so retries do not unexpectedly change content after a template or preference update. Sensitive notifications should contain minimal private data and link back to an authenticated product view.

Fanout can be per event or per recipient. A small direct message can create one job per recipient synchronously in a queue transaction. A high-fanout announcement should create a campaign record and expand recipients in batches to avoid a huge write burst. Partition by recipient or campaign, apply rate and concurrency limits per channel, and prioritize time-sensitive transactional messages separately from bulk marketing. Deduplicate recipients and events before delivery, while preserving product-required semantics such as every security alert.

## Channel adapters and delivery state

Workers consume jobs and call provider adapters. Each job stores channel, destination token reference, template version, attempt count, next retry time, and state. Provider timeouts are ambiguous: the provider may have accepted the message even when the response was lost. Use a provider idempotency key when available, otherwise accept possible duplicate delivery and design messages so duplicates are tolerable. Retry transient failures with bounded exponential backoff and jitter; permanent invalid-address or unregistered-device responses should deactivate the endpoint. Dead-letter persistent failures with an operator replay path.

Mobile push systems are best effort. FCM notes that acceptance does not mean delivery and delivery order is not guaranteed; collapse keys can replace older queued notifications. APNs may reorder, throttle, delay, or store notifications according to expiration and device conditions. Therefore, push should usually be a hint to fetch authoritative state from the app backend, not the only durable record of an event. In-app notifications can be persisted in a user inbox and queried on reconnect; email and SMS require provider feedback loops for bounce, complaint, and delivery status.

## Preferences, abuse, and operations

Store device registrations with user, platform, token, last-seen time, and invalidation state. Tokens rotate, so clients need registration refresh. Respect opt-outs and channel-specific consent. Protect provider credentials and rate limits; bulk sends can trigger provider throttling or cost spikes. Make quiet hours, localization, unsubscribe, and emergency override policy explicit.

Measure queue age, time from event to provider acceptance, delivery callback rate, bounce and complaint rate, duplicate rate, invalid tokens, provider throttling, and per-campaign spend. Track each lifecycle state accurately: queued, attempted, accepted, delivered if reported, opened if instrumented, or failed. Test provider outage, invalid credentials, a million-recipient campaign, preference changes during retry, and duplicate events. A reliable system preserves intent and gives honest observability even when the final device or inbox is outside its control.

## Design review

Is business state committed atomically with notification intent? How are campaigns expanded without a thundering herd? What does “delivered” mean per provider? How are ambiguous retries and token rotation handled? Can one bulk send starve transactional notifications?
