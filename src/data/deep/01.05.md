Availability, durability, consistency, and cost describe different promises. They are often grouped together because a change to one affects the others, but they should be measured separately. A service can be reachable while returning stale data; it can be temporarily unreachable while preserving every accepted write.

## Define each promise at the user boundary

**Availability** asks whether a valid operation succeeds when attempted. Define the operation and denominator: a read API may be available while the checkout writer is down. **Durability** asks whether acknowledged data survives specified failures. **Consistency** asks which versions of data an operation may observe and in what order. **Cost** includes infrastructure, engineering time, and operational work needed to maintain the promise.

Consider a photo-sharing service. A user uploads a photo and receives success. If the image is still only in a process buffer, a crash may lose it: the durability promise was overstated. If metadata is durable but the image copy is still propagating, a feed read may show a placeholder: availability and consistency are separate. If every feed read waits for global propagation, the service may become slower and more expensive than the product needs.

## Match guarantees to the operation

| Operation | Useful promise | Possible implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acknowledge upload | Image and metadata survive one host loss | Durable object write plus metadata commit protocol |
| View own new photo | Read your write | Route to authoritative state or session-aware cache |
| View public feed | Bounded staleness may be acceptable | Asynchronous fanout or derived feed |
| Delete photo | Stop serving it promptly | Tombstone and invalidation before physical cleanup |

These are examples, not universal rules. A regulated record may require stronger durability and auditability. A live “people typing” indicator may tolerate complete loss. State the failure scenario for each guarantee: one process crash, one zone failure, a regional outage, or accidental deletion. “Durable” without a failure model is too vague.

## Account for the hidden cost of stronger guarantees

Synchronous replication can reduce acknowledged-write loss, but makes success depend on more network and storage components. Geographic replication can improve disaster recovery, but cross-region acknowledgment adds latency. Stronger read consistency may require coordination with the leader or quorum, which can reduce read availability during partitions. Retaining many copies lowers some risks while increasing storage cost and deletion complexity.

Do not interpret the CAP theorem as “pick any two of three” for an ordinary healthy system. Its central issue appears during a network partition: if nodes cannot communicate, a design must define whether operations continue with potentially divergent state or are refused until coordination is restored. Outside that failure, latency, replication lag, and operational complexity are still important design dimensions.

## Set a cost-aware reliability target

Google SRE describes an error budget as the allowed amount of bad service implied by an SLO. A 99.9% request-success objective permits at most 0.1% bad eligible requests during the window. Moving to 99.99% leaves ten times less failure budget. That can require more isolation, automation, and testing. The extra reliability should be tied to a user or business need, not a round-number preference.

Cost should be measured as a unit economics question as well as a monthly bill: cost per uploaded GB, per thousand feed reads, or per active user. A design that is cheap at small scale may have poor marginal cost if every write is copied to every user feed. Estimate both the fixed baseline and the cost added by one more unit of work.

## Design review

When a write succeeds, what exact failures can happen next without losing it? During a partition, which operations continue? Which views may be stale, for how long, and how does a user learn that? What is the recurring cost of each extra guarantee?
