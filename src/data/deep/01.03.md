Non-functional requirements turn “the system should be fast and reliable” into constraints that can guide design and operations. They describe the quality of a feature from a user's perspective: latency, availability, durability, security, scale, recovery, and cost. A target only helps when its unit, population, and time window are explicit.

## Express quality as a measurable promise

For a document-upload API, “fast” might mean “99% of successful requests under 500 ms for files below 10 MB, measured at the API edge over 28 days.” This leaves room to specify a separate target for large uploads. A single average hides slow users: a 100 ms median can coexist with a 15-second tail. Google's SRE guidance defines a **service level indicator** as a measured aspect of service and a **service level objective** as its target. Choose indicators that reflect a real user journey.

Availability also needs a denominator. “99.9% available” could mean time with a healthy process, successful API requests, or completed user tasks. Request-based availability is often more useful for a service with partial failures. Exclude only clearly documented classes, such as invalid client requests; otherwise a service can appear healthy by redefining failures away.

## Give each requirement a failure model

Requirements are most useful when they imply behavior during failure. A durability target asks what happens if a host dies immediately after a successful write. Recovery point objective (RPO) bounds acceptable data loss; recovery time objective (RTO) bounds how long restoration may take. A backup that has never been restored does not establish either target. For multi-region replication, decide whether acknowledgment waits for the remote copy or whether a regional disaster may lose recent writes.

| Quality | Concrete question | Likely design effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Latency | Which percentile and request class? | Indexes, caches, bounded sync path |
| Availability | What user operation must succeed? | Redundancy, failover, graceful degradation |
| Durability | What write loss is acceptable? | Replication, log, backups, restore drills |
| Scale | What peak rate and growth horizon? | Capacity headroom and partition plan |
| Security | Who may read or change which data? | Auth, audit, encryption, isolation |
| Cost | What monthly envelope and unit cost? | Tier choice, retention, autoscaling |

Avoid assuming every dimension requires the maximum possible guarantee. An internal dashboard may tolerate a few minutes of stale data; a money transfer record should not. Separate critical operations from convenience features. This allows a design to preserve core actions during a dependency outage while degrading secondary views.

## Measure the complete path

An API can meet its own latency target while the user's workflow remains slow because of DNS, a downstream service, client retries, or rendering. Define whether the target is measured at the browser, edge, service, or worker. Add instrumentation at the chosen boundary and keep a breakdown for diagnosis. For queued work, measure end-to-end age from acceptance to outcome, not just worker execution time.

SLOs imply an **error budget**. Over a 28-day window, 99.9% success permits 0.1% bad events, or 1,000 per million eligible requests. That budget gives teams a concrete way to decide whether to ship changes or focus on stability. It does not mean intentionally failing requests; it acknowledges that reliability work has a cost and should be tied to user value.

## Resolve conflicting targets

Low latency and strong consistency can conflict when writes must cross distant regions. Durability and cost can conflict when retaining multiple copies for years. A design review should record the priority for each user journey and the allowed fallback. For instance, a checkout may reject when payment status is unknown, while the order-history page may show a delayed update. Treat these as separate promises rather than forcing one blanket guarantee on the whole product.

## Design review

Can an operator evaluate each requirement from telemetry during a bad day? If a statement lacks a unit, percentile, population, location, or measurement window, rewrite it until two teams would calculate the same result.
