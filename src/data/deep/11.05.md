Feed generation selects posts from accounts a user follows and orders them for display. The central tradeoff is when to do the distribution work. Push fanout writes a post reference into follower inboxes at publish time. Pull fanout reads recent posts from followed accounts when a user opens the feed. A hybrid uses both for different author sizes or freshness needs. The choice depends on follower skew, read frequency, ranking complexity, and acceptable delay.

## Compare push and pull costs

If an ordinary author has 200 followers, inserting 200 feed references may be cheap and make each follower's feed read fast. If a celebrity has ten million followers, one post can create ten million writes and a long queue. Pull avoids that publish burst by storing the post once, but a user following thousands of accounts may require many reads and a large merge on every feed open. The approximate load is not just total users; examine the distribution of followers and followed accounts, post rate, and feed-open rate.

A hybrid pushes ordinary authors and fetches high-follower authors on read. At read time, merge precomputed inbox entries with pulled posts, deduplicate by post ID, apply visibility checks, then rank and page. The threshold should be chosen from measured write and read costs, not a fixed folklore number. A celebrity might also have extremely active followers, so no single threshold works for every product.

## Keep the source and projection separate

Store the authoritative post once with author, timestamp, visibility, and version. An inbox entry contains a post ID and metadata for candidate retrieval. A fanout job must be restartable and idempotent with a unique `(viewer_id, post_id)` key. Record progress through follower pages so a crash does not require starting from zero. FlockDB's original design notes show why paging huge follower lists was an explicit requirement for tweet delivery.

Follow relationships can change while fanout runs. Decide whether a new follower receives older posts and whether an unfollow removes existing inbox entries. Regardless of denormalized entries, apply current blocks, privacy, and moderation state at read time. A deleted post must disappear even if stale references remain in inbox storage. Maintain a repair job that can reconstruct inbox entries from posts and relationships within a bounded history window.

## Rank and serve with a stable cursor

A chronological feed can page by timestamp and post ID. A ranked feed may mix freshness, relevance, and interactions, so scores can change between pages. Use a cursor tied to a ranking snapshot or accept documented best-effort pagination with possible movement. Fetch more candidates than the display page needs because deduplication and visibility filtering will remove some. Cache the first page for active users only when invalidation and privacy are correct.

Measure publish-to-visible lag by author class, fanout queue depth, feed-open p95 latency, missing-entry repair rate, and privacy-filter rejections. Test a celebrity publish burst and a user who follows thousands of accounts. The system should keep publishing responsive even when fanout is delayed, and the UI should not imply that every follower has received a post as soon as the author's write succeeds.

## Design review

What are the follower and followee count distributions? Which authors are pushed versus pulled? How does fanout resume after partial completion? Is visibility enforced at read time? How stable are pages when ranking and relationships change?
